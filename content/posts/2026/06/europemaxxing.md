With the heatwave continuing its firm grip on central Europe, I'm traveling through Denmark and Germany
for a wedding and an impossible sports event.

<!--more-->

{{< img src="2026/06/europemaxxing/cph-hafen-rutsche.avif" alt="An improvised waterslide around the Copenhagen harbour area" >}}

As I already indicated in my last post, the last week of June is a turbulent one for me.
I handed in my laptop at my previous job last Wednesday before starting my new one July 1st.
The one week I have between jobs is spent traveling: From Hamburg to Copenhagen, to celebrate with my
freshly-married friends; back to Hamburg, to pack and grab a good night's sleep; on to Frankfurt,
to cheer on another friend who's taking part in a long-awaited Ironman.

While all of this is happening, temperatures in Germany are reaching record-highs. In some places,
we're beating 40ºC, temperatures unheard of for June. And neither Germany nor Denmark have the infrastructure
to deal with these temperatures: The most glaring issue is, of course, the lack of air conditioning.
Any country that experiences this kind of weather regularly has AC just about everywhere you go, which makes
the heat much more bearable. Additionally, water is understood to be a vital resource, and you can get it cheaply
anywhere you go. Germany? €2 for half a liter of water at the kiosk. Pay up or perish.

What's more, it is currently midsummer, and the harsh sunlight is ever-present for over fifteen hours a day,
from around 6 in the morning all the way until around 9 in the evening. There is no respite from the sun,
and the concrete never quite cools down.

Still, people try to live their lives the best they can. In Copenhagen specifically, I was incredibly impressed
with the degree of work-life balance that enabled people to hang out in the parks or go for a swim in the
harbor area in the middle of the day. I've tried to capture, as best as I can, the spirit of people making the most of 
the current situation.

Another park with a very different vibe is Planten un Blomen – I went there to catch what will likely

Afterward, I went to the Dammtor train station, which is close by. My train was only delayed by an hour (shoutout
to the Deutsche Bahn!), and I managed to catch a few hours of sleep before getting to Copenhagen at 6:30 the next day.

## Copenhagen

Copenhagen turned out to be an incredible city. I had been there very briefly over ten years ago, but didn't really
get to appreciate the city. This time, my visit was equally brief – I spent just over 24 hours there, but I did make
the most of it this time around.

{{< img src="2026/06/europemaxxing/cph-fahrraeder.avif" alt="I love the bicycle infrastructure in Copenhagen." >}}

It wasn't as hot as it had been in Hamburg. At a comparatively mild 26°C, you could still walk around easily,
and life mostly seemed to be proceeding as usual, although people noticeably moved to wherever there was water.
