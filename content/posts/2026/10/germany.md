When I tell people that I'm currently living as a digital nomad, the usual reaction is "Oh, that's great, I'd love to do that as well!".
I think what most people *imagine* when they think about being a digital nomad is working from the beach in Bali with a cold beer right
beside them. 

<!-- more -->

But that's not what we're doing. We're staying in Germany, in a different city every month, with a dog. It's not the kind
of adventure that people sometimes imagine. Don't get me wrong, it's still awesome and I love it! I'm just making
excuses for not having great pictures to post.

I struggled to write this post because there wasn't really anything exciting going on.
After returning from New York, we briefly stayed in Sweden for a week, but shortly returned to Leipzig and then Bochum,
for around two months total. We'd also returned to a boring routine: Because we want to be in shape for our wedding next March,
we've started hitting the gym again, something not particularly easy to do when you constantly switch places.

Meanwhile, Summer has turned to Autumn in Germany, and it's looking less appetizing outside by the day.
All of that to say that between more rigid routines, shorter days and less spectacular sights, my photography
has suffered. That said: I'm typing this post from Malaga, so expect a more visually pleasing post sometime soon.
This edition, however, contains pictures taken mid-August in Sweden, September in Leipzig and October in Bochum.

## Bochum

The reason we came to Bochum was that Liza's sister, Anya, moved there from Moscow to study computer science.
We figured we'd swing by so we could help out and support her in adjusting. It turned out that she was doing
just fine and as a matter of fact didn't really want a whole lot to do with us.

Bochum wasn't the most pleasant of sights. It's located in an industrial melting pot of cities called the
"Ruhrgebiet" ("Ruhr area"), named imaginatively after the river that flows through it. The Ruhrgebiet has by
far the highest concentration of people within Germany; due to its industrial importance, it was heavily destroyed
during World War 2, and unfortunately aesthetics were not the chief priority when it was rebuilt after the war.

What I am getting at is that the whole Ruhrgebiet is, no matter where you go, the same kind of ugly everywhere.
The nature is quite pleasant: There are lush, green forests and rolling hills when you get outside the cities.
