In early August, we went to New York for a week to attend the wedding (technically, the one-year anniversary)
of a school friend of Liza's. Of course, I could not let that opportunity to take some awesome shots pass me by.

<!-- more -->

Incidentally, we had been in New York almost exactly one year prior, to visit the same friends.
They had just gotten married, but in a relatively unspectacular fashion, and without a big celebration.
So when we got invited to join their celebration this year, we were more than happy to come again.

Returning and already kind of _knowing_ our way around was an incredibly nice feeling. Our main aim for the
one-week visit was to fill in some gaps and do the things we couldn't make the time for during our previous visit,
and doing some of the things we enjoyed the most *again*.

{{< carousel base="2026/08/new-york" images="new-york-01, new-york-02" alt="I have a different post on black-white architecture lined up, some day." >}}

I'm not going to try to document everything we did in New York; it'd be way too much to type out, and easily three
to four times the length of my usual posts. I really did take more pictures in a week than I usually do in a month.
This is going to be a best-of, with some recommendations if you ever visit NY as well :)

### The High Line

Our hotel was on West 38th Street, close to Times Square and Broadway. It is also close to [The High Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Line),
a defunct railway track stretching for over 2km across West Manhattan. It was one of our highlights the last time we went,
so we were eager to go again. 

We went on our very first morning in the city, but ran into some very nasty weather and got completely soaked.
Because my camera is not fully weather-sealed, no pictures from that attempt. However, we did try again the evening
after and walked from our hotel to [Little Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Island_at_Pier_55) via the High Line.

### The Rockefeller Building

One of the major tourist attractions we didn't do last year was visiting any kind of observation deck.
Between the Empire State Building, the Top of the Rock, One World Observatory, The Edge and probably a few more,
there's quite the variety to choose from. We ended up going to the Top of the Rock, which we very much enjoyed.

The overcast sky gives some character to the pictures, I think.

{{< carousel base="2026/08/new-york" images="new-york-08, new-york-10" alt="The Empire State Building: with overcast skies, and then some sun" >}}

I was fascinated to learn that the famous picture [Lunch atop a Skyscraper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunch_atop_a_Skyscraper)
was created during the construction of Rockefeller Center, which hosts the Top of the Rock.
In that spirit, they have a steel beam up there that you can get on for a quick photo op of your own.
We didn't go, but I captured what I thought was a very sweet moment.

### More Manhattan

After leaving Rockefeller Center, we headed to Central Park. That's one of the places we had really enjoyed the first time around
and were happy to return to. It was super hot, so we didn't walk as much as we would have liked, but we did get to catch a baseball
game, which felt very  🦅🇺🇸American🇺🇸🦅.

{{< carousel base="2026/08/new-york" images="new-york-12,new-york-13,new-york-14,new-york-15" alt="A baseball game in Central Park" >}}

Afterwards we went to the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA), which is another thing we would've liked to do the first time around
but couldn't fit into our museum schedule). We had done [The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/de) last time, and I would have liked to
to go again, but each of these can easily take half a day. I think we spent around three to four hours at MoMA and explored maybe
60-70% of it. It's just that massive.

I don't have a lot of pictures here, although the art there was quite fascinating. Here's a Van Gogh though.

### All the Islands!

Honestly, just Staten Island and Roosevelt Island.

The second day, we revisited Roosevelt Island. It's a small oasis on the Hudson river between Manhattan and Queens,
and you can reach it three different ways: via the subway, boat or a very scenic gondola ride. The gondola is always crowded,
but we still opted for it because hey, we're still tourists!

One funny factoid about the gondola is that it is located next to 59th Street, about which there is a
well-known [Simon and Garfunkel song](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=So0ZrTwf8vI). I'll never **not** listen to it
when I'm in the area.

{{< img src="2026/08/new-york/new-york-21.avif" alt="If you don't know the song, do yourself a favor and listen to it :)" >}}

We didn't really see a lot on the island. We primarily wanted to visit the 
[Franklin D. Roosevelt Four Freedoms State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt_Four_Freedoms_Park),
because last time we ended up coming just as the park was closing, and we took that personally.
This time we got there at 10am! Unfortunately, we didn't check the opening hours and it turns out they're
closed on Tuesdays. It was a Tuesday. Oh well.
We probably should have checked in advance. If we ever come back: third time's gonna be the charm.

{{< img src="2026/08/new-york/new-york-20.avif" alt="Roosevelt Island is such a nice place to get away from the hustle and bustle of Manhattan" >}}

Staten Island is a different story. We really wanted to take the ferries and take the chance to see the Statue of Liberty.
I also wanted to go see the home of the [Wu-Tang](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PBwAxmrE194). 
Unfortunately we didn't really have enough time to walk across Staten Island to find murals, so you'll have to settle for the Statue of Liberty.

### Times Square

Times Square at night! We had already been, and I must say that *I do not like it*. It's loud, it's crowded, and you basically
cannot stop listening to [Empire State of Mind](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vk6014HuxcE) because it's blasting at every corner.
I'm not exaggerating this, it's genuinely everywhere. Not hating, it's a great song, for the first fifty times or so :D

I don't have a lot of pictures because we didn't spend a lot of time there. We just went in, put our headphones on noise cancelling,
walked through the chaos once, took the pictures we needed to take and left.
