In early August, we went to New York for a week to attend the wedding (technically, the one-year anniversary)
of a school friend of Liza's. Of course, I could not let that opportunity to take some awesome shots pass me by.

<!-- more -->

---

Incidentally, we had been in New York almost exactly one year prior, to visit Liza's friends for their actual
wedding. They had just gotten married, but in a relatively unspectacular fashion, and without a big celebration.
The celebration happened this year, and so we didn't pass up the chance of visiting this great city again.

It was an incredible feeling of returning and already _knowing_ a bunch of places. Coming back to re-visit
some of the places we had enjoyed the most. Being able to visit the places we hadn't found the time for
the last time around.

The weather wasn't exactly playing nice during our stay: It was gray and rainy the entire time, and it was
~30°C with around 80% humidity almost the entire time, meaning we were absolutely drenched within five minutes
of leaving our hotel. Liza remarked something that I thought was interesting though: Because you're surrounded
by skyscrapers the entire time, you don't even notice the grey sky that much because you *just don't see it*.
I thought that was fascinating.

---

Our days were usually quite ambitious, with us aiming for around 20000 steps every day. Sometimes, we tried to be lazy and still
made it to around 17000. We seriously need to learn how to relax.

The following is not a full itinerary for New York. It's not even close. It just happens to be the things we couldn't get around to 
the first time, and the things we enjoyed so much we wanted to do them again.

Our hotel was on West 38th Street, close to Times Square and Broadway. It is also close to [The High Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Line),
a defunct railway track stretching for over 2km across West Manhattan. It was one of our highlights the last time we went,
so we were eager to go again.

### 04. August

The second day, we started out at Roosevelt Island. It's a small oasis on the Hudson river between Manhattan and Queens,
and you can reach it three different ways: via the subway, boat or a very scenic gondola ride. The gondola is always crowded,
but we still opted for it because hey, we're still tourists!

One funny thing about the gondola is that the way to reach it is via 59th Street.
As someone who has listened to a lot of Simon and Garfunkel, I am always reminded of the [59th Street Song](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=So0ZrTwf8vI) 
anytime I'm headed to that station.

{{< img src="2026/08/new-york/new-york-20.avif" alt="Roosevelt Island is such a nice place to get away from the hustle and bustle of Manhattan" >}}

{{< img src="2026/08/new-york/new-york-21.avif" alt="If you don't know the song, do yourself a favor and listen to it :)" >}}

On a side note, this is something I really love about New York: There's just *so much music* about it. It's like every other street or area
has its own song. Being a bit of a music buff (some would say), it's a thoroughly enjoyable experience to find a place and realise that there's
a song about it. The less enjoyable part is getting blasted with [Jay-Z](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vk6014HuxcE) on
every other corner around Times Square (great song, for the first fifty times or so).
